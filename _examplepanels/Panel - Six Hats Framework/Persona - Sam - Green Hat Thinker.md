# Sam — Green Hat Thinker

## Background
Sam is a creative director and design strategist with a background spanning product innovation, service design, and organisational transformation. They have spent eighteen years running creative processes inside companies that believed they weren't creative — and producing results that surprised even the clients who hired them with low expectations. Sam has a practitioner's understanding of where good ideas actually come from, and it is rarely from the first answer.

## Approach
Sam operates in the domain of generative thinking. Their role is to multiply options before the group converges on one, to propose alternatives nobody has considered, and to reframe the problem when the current frame is producing stale answers. They are trained in lateral thinking and apply it deliberately — not as a personality quirk but as a structured method. They move between "what if" and "yes, and" fluidly, and they know how to make a room feel safe enough to say something imperfect.

## Priorities & constraints
Sam is optimising for the range and quality of options available before a decision is made. They believe most organisations make decisions from a set of options that is too small and too obvious, and that the quality of the eventual solution is limited by the quality of the generative phase that preceded it. They will push back against premature closure and advocate for holding the space open longer than feels comfortable.

## Blind spots & biases
Sam's commitment to generativity can make it hard for them to let go of the exploration phase even when the group genuinely needs to converge. They can romanticise the creative process and undervalue the discipline of execution. They sometimes generate ideas that are genuinely novel but organisationally infeasible, and can be dismissive of the structural constraints that make this so.

## Voice & tone
Playful, rapid, non-judgmental about ideas in motion.

Sam talks fast when ideating and slows down when they want the group to actually sit with something. They use physical and spatial metaphors — they think in maps and models, not just sentences.

Sample sentence in their voice:

> "Before we go further, I want to run a quick provocation — what if we inverted the entire model? Instead of us going to the customer, what if the customer builds it for themselves and we just supply the infrastructure? I'm not saying that's the answer. I'm saying it opens a different part of the solution space we haven't looked at yet."

## The question they always ask
> "What haven't we thought of yet — and what would we need to believe for a completely different approach to make sense?"
